# School Games Hub: privacy summary

*For the principal and board of Douglas Park School. Checked against the code and the live database, 15 October 2026.*

School Games Hub (https://bleedingrobot.github.io/school-games-hub/) is a chess and checkers website for Years 4–6, run by James. Students sign in with their school Google account, pick a made-up player name, build a cartoon avatar, and play computer opponents or each other.

**What is stored, and why**

- **Our database:** a random ID; a player name from word lists (e.g. "Speedy Otter 27"); avatar choices; student or teacher; hidden or not; when the account was made. For each game: the players' random IDs, the moves, the result and the time. Points, badges and achievements. *Why:* profiles without real names, checking nobody cheats, and the points. No real names, emails, photos or free text. (Since F.2: which school the student is in, and which class and group, as a teacher-typed name like "Room 12" with no student's name in it. Class names are shown only to the students and teachers of that class, after a correct Class Warp code, and never on a leaderboard.)
- **Gem Keep (times tables, since 17 Oct 2026):** each level played (stars, waves reached, how many maths questions were asked and answered right), the best stars and Level Keys per level, and **learning data**: for each times-table fact (e.g. 7 × 8), how often it was asked and answered right, the average answer time, a mastery box (0–4) and the last two days it was answered quickly. Never the answers typed. *Why:* so progress follows the student between devices, the game asks the facts they need, and teachers can see which facts a student (or the class) finds hard. **Since 17 Oct 2026, space games add to the same times-table records** when Mission Control maths is on (the teacher can switch it off): each question asked in a space game and whether it was answered right, never the typed answer.
- **Marble Rush (once it is live):** each student's best time on each course, with the recorded steering of their best runs so they can be played back as a "ghost", and a record of each saved run (time, falls, when; no steering). The fastest-time boards show player names (made-up word names) and avatars only, and only to the same school; hidden students and teachers are left out. With maths on, answers at gates, pit stops and rescues go into the same maths records as the other games (asked, right or not; never the typed answer). Number gems and "guess your time" are not saved. *Why:* best times, ghosts and boards, and so the game asks the maths the student needs.
- **Basic facts Training and Quick looks (once live):** each practice answer: which fact or picture (e.g. 7 × 8, or "ten-frame, 8"), whether it was right, the day and time, and for times tables how long it took and how long that student takes to type; for Quick looks nothing is timed and nothing is typed (taps only). Plus the student's typing check times and a few settings (auto-send, where the Quick looks starting check put them, the stage reached, "keep the picture on the screen"). The newest 30 answers of each fact or picture are kept. Only the student can see their own, and teachers see them the way they see Training's (one student at a time); nothing is shown to classmates, there are no leaderboards or points, and it does not go into the games' maths records. *Why:* so the facts map and the garden follow the student between devices, and sessions practise what they need.
- **Training before games (once live):** when a student finishes a whole Training session or Quick looks round, the time it finished and how many answers it had. Only used to open the games for 2 hours in a class whose teacher switched "Training before games" on; nobody else sees it. *Why:* so a session done on one Chromebook opens the games on another.
- **Bond Match:** each finished game (the bond practised, board size, the card flips in order, flips, time, stars, and whether it was played alone, by two players on one device or against the computer), and each student's best flips, time and stars per bond and board size. Only the student and their own teachers can see them; nothing is shown to classmates and nothing goes into the maths records. In a two-player game only the signed-in student is saved (the other player isn't recorded). *Why:* stars and bests, and the small capped points.
- **Mine Runner (once it is live):** each student's best time on each of the six mines (and its hard version, with and without maths questions), with the recorded steering of their best run; a record of each saved run (mine, time, how many doors and pads were opened by an answer, how many wrong answers at question doors, and the recorded steering, which is thrown away after 7 days); and the question list made for each run with maths (thrown away after 2 days, never visible to students). Each answer also goes into the student's normal maths records, as in other games. Students can see their own times and a school board with the ten fastest in their own school: display name, avatar and time only, never anyone's wrong answers or maths results. Teachers see when a student last played. Nothing is shared with other schools. *Why:* best times and boards, small capped points and achievements.
- **Archery Challenge (once it is live):** each student's coins, the arrows, helpers, shields and bows they own, coins spent in all, and their best fight score in each world; achievement counts (fights won in each world, wins with only Plain Arrows, wins against the Hard bot, perfect speed rounds, classmate fights played and won: numbers only). A New Season wipes all of these; earned achievements stay. Speed-round questions and when each was sent, with the answers marked (thrown away after 2 days, never visible to students); each answer also goes into the student's normal maths records, as in other games. Fights against the computer (seed, world, the bot's level, what was packed, how it ended). "Who's here": the last time each student had the game open (thrown away after a day), so classmates in the **same class** can see their display name and avatar while it's open. Classmate fights: both players' display names, what each packed, and the turns played, seen only by those two players (thrown away after 30 days). Never anyone's coins or maths results shown to classmates. Teachers see when a student last played and a class summary of counts only (pupils who played this week, fights finished, speed rounds today). Nothing is shared with other schools. *Why:* the Armory (coins come only from maths), fair fights checked by the server, classmate fights, small capped points and achievements.
- **Kingdom Keeper (once it is live):** each student's reigns: the ruler picture they chose, their city year by year (people, grain, land, what happened each year), and how each reign ended (rating, points, people at the end). This year's council questions with the answers marked (never visible to students; each answer also goes into the student's normal maths records, as in other games), and a count of council answers right (for an achievement). A New Season wipes all reigns; earned achievements stay. Nothing is shown to classmates. Teachers see when a student last played. Nothing is shared with other schools. *Why:* the game is saved between visits and checked by the server, small capped points and achievements.
- **Clan Quest made-up hero names (once it is live, 27 Oct 2026):** only if a teacher switches "Made-up hero names" on for their class (it is off unless they do). A pupil may then type a name for each of their four heroes instead of picking one from the list: letters only, up to 12, checked for rude words by the server before it is saved, and kept with the clan until the clan is reset. Only the pupil and their own teachers see hero names (on the clan page and the teacher's view of that pupil); classmates never do, and clan names still come from word lists. Teachers can swap a pupil's made-up names back to list names with one button. *Why:* the class asked to name their own heroes. Pupils should be reminded not to use their own or a classmate's real name.
- **Maths focus results (added with F.8, once it is live):** when a teacher sets a maths focus, for each student the focus is for, three running totals: how many questions they answered on the focus's skills while it ran, how many were right, and how many were right and quick. Not the questions, not the answers typed, not when each was answered. *Why:* so the teacher can see how the class did during a focus. Only that class's teachers can see it; the student cannot.
- **Supabase's sign-in records:** school email, name from Google, a link to the Google profile photo, the Google account number, last sign-in time, and each signed-in device's internet (IP) address and browser type. *Why:* Supabase needs these to run Google sign-in. The site shows only the part of the email before the @ (the student's real name), and only on the teacher page.
- **Supabase's logs:** each request's IP address, rough location (city) and browser, deleted after 1 day.
- **The student's browser:** their sign-in session and whether sound is on. Signing out clears the session. For Gem Keep, a copy of their own progress and anything not yet saved (so it works through a Wi-Fi drop).

**Where it is (Privacy Principle 12)**

The database and server code are in **Sydney, Australia**, run by Supabase (a US company) in Amazon data centres. The live logs show every request entering through Auckland and running in Sydney. The website files come from GitHub Pages (US), which stores no student data. Sign-in happens inside the school's own Google Workspace, as an "Internal" app only Douglas Park accounts can use. A cloud service that only stores data for us is usually not a "disclosure" (Privacy Act 2020, section 11), but the school stays responsible for its security (Principle 5). The school's privacy officer should confirm this is acceptable.

**Who can see what**

- **Students:** their own games and points, and other students' player names, avatars and badges (boards, lobby, opponents). Never emails or real names. Database rules stop them reading other students' games or changing anything except their own name and avatar. This was tested.
- **Teachers:** (since F.2, only the students in their own classes; a school admin sees the whole school; nobody sees another school) every player's name, avatar, games, points and last 10 results. They can give a new random name, hide, delete, give teacher access, pause games against friends and start a new season. Since 16 Oct 2026 they also see each student's real name (the part of their school email before the @) next to the player name, so they know who is who. Nothing else from the email or Google account. For Gem Keep they see each student's levels, stars and a 12 × 12 times-table grid (not tried / learning / secure / fluent), the class's 10 hardest facts, and they set Gem Keep's maths settings for everyone. Students never see anyone's maths: the Gem Keep leaderboard shows stars only.
- **James (account owner):** everything, through the Supabase and GitHub dashboards, including sign-in records and the 1-day logs.

**Deleting**

- **One student:** Teacher page → the student → **Delete** → type their player name. This removes their sign-in record (email, name, devices), profile, games, points and achievements. Opponents keep the shared game, with nothing pointing back. Logs expire within a day. There are no backups, so it can't be undone.
- **End of year:** delete leavers, then type `NEW SEASON`. That clears every finished game, point, rating and achievement, all Gem Keep progress and times-table records, and all Marble Rush times and runs, keeping accounts, names and avatars.

**Deliberately not there:** ads, tracking, analytics, outside fonts, chat or messages of any kind, free typing, photo uploads. Computer opponents run on the student's own Chromebook.

**Honest limits of the free services**

- No uptime promise and no official support. If Supabase has a problem, the site stops until it's fixed (saved games are safe).
- It sleeps after a week unused (e.g. holidays). Waking it is one click and loses nothing.
- If a usage limit were passed (a class uses a small part of them), Supabase warns, then may slow or stop the site until the next month. No card is on file, so there can't be a bill.
- Free terms can change. James would tell the school before anything moved or cost money.
- Games against the computer happen on the student's device, so a determined student could fake one. They earn only small, capped points and never affect rankings.
- The code is public on GitHub. It contains no passwords, keys or student data.

1. Approve the site under the school's privacy policy, including storage in Australia.
2. Decide whether to tell parents (a short newsletter note is suggested: school accounts, made-up names, no chat).
3. Name a second person who can look after the site if James is away.
4. Agree the yearly routine: delete leavers, then start a new season.

Nothing more is needed from the Google Workspace admin: the app is "Internal" and students already sign in without extra approval.
