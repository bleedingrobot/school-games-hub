# Star Forge: painted pictures plan

Every painting for the Star Forge (`docs/TRADER-SYSTEM-PLAN.md`, section 6), ready for James to paint on the gaming PC. It follows `docs/TRADER-ART-PLAN.md` (the Cinematic style, the review page, the child-safety check), so most of the tools already exist.

Written 3 November 2026 for James. **Status: ready to paint.** Nothing has been painted yet.

## The short version

- **229 pictures to paint**, in three batches. The painter script makes them all by itself, one after another, on the gaming PC. Roughly an hour of painting in all (section 4 has the guesses per batch).
- **Batch 1 is the one the viewer needs most:** 60 planet surface maps (15 kinds × 4 surfaces), 4 cloud maps, 8 suns and 8 skies. With just batch 1 the Forge looks properly painted.
- **Batch 2:** 5 ring strips, 8 moons, 10 night maps (city lights and lava glow).
- **Batch 3:** 31 life postcards, 22 technology pictures, 73 Forge button pictures (and 68 more buttons cut from the planet and sky maps for free).
- **Same style as the planets kids know:** Cinematic. Same painter (Z-Image Turbo), same settings as Star Trader and Clan Quest, so a Forge planet sits next to a Star Trader planet without looking odd.
- **Bump maps are made from the pictures.** Nobody paints them. The tidy-up script turns each planet picture into a height map and then a normal map (section 5).
- **One try each.** Claude checks every picture, remakes only the bad ones, then shows James the set on a review page.
- **Every painting is optional in the game.** A missing one falls back to a picture drawn in code, so batches can go live one at a time.

## 2. How the pictures are painted

- **The painter:** `scripts/trader-forge-art-gen.py`. It sends each picture to ComfyUI on the gaming PC (`http://100.97.96.115:8188`, over Tailscale) and saves a PNG in `~/dragon-art-inbox/trader-forge-art/<group>/<name>.png`. It skips pictures already there, so a run that stops can just be started again. The prompt is stored inside each PNG (ComfyUI's own copy, and a plain `forge_prompt` entry).
- **The settings are Star Trader's and Clan Quest's:** Z-Image Turbo (`z_image_turbo_int8_convrot`), the Qwen 3 4B text model, 8 steps, `res_multistep`, then a 1.5× second pass at strength 0.4. Seeds are fixed from the picture's name, so any picture can be painted again exactly.
- **The original Star Trader painter wasn't found.** `~/dragon-art-inbox/tools/trader-art.py` is gone (the folder only holds `diamond-art`, `maths-monsters-final` and `maths-monsters-quests`). The graph was copied from `scripts/diamond-art.py` (the Clan Quest graph), and the planet-map size from `TRADER-ART-PLAN.md` (2304 × 1152). The style words are Claude's wording of "Cinematic film concept art", not James's exact old prompt, so look at the first few planets against the old ones.
- **There is no negative prompt.** This model runs with the negative side switched off, and it paints whatever a prompt names, even after "no". So every prompt says only what is there ("ground and surface only", "plain pure black background"), never "no stars". Where the plan asked for a negative prompt, the wording is built into the prompt instead. If a map comes back as a ball or with stars, remake it (section 6), and if it keeps happening Claude changes the wording.
- **Prompt shape.** Each prompt is `style + the picture's own words + a tail` for its group. The tails are what make a planet a flat map and not a ball:
  - **Planet, moon and sun maps:** "Seamless flat top-down surface texture map of …, seen from directly above as one flat rectangular texture map that fills the whole picture edge to edge, wide 2 to 1 shape, the left and right edges continue into each other, even soft light with no shadows, the same amount of detail everywhere, ground and surface only."
  - **Skies:** "A seamless 360 degree equirectangular panorama of deep space, wide 2 to 1 shape … only sharp tiny stars and glowing space gas."
  - **Clouds:** "bright white cloud on a solid pure black background, every part without cloud is plain pure black."
  - **Night maps:** "tiny bright glowing lights scattered on a solid pure black background."
  - **Rings:** "straight parallel vertical stripes from the left edge to the right edge … the gaps between stripes are plain pure black." (The tidy script averages the stripes into one line.)
  - **Postcards:** "Wide cinematic landscape painting, warm glowing light, friendly and wondrous … suitable for children aged 8 to 12."
  - **Cut-outs:** "on a plain pure white background" (rembg cuts them out). **Textures:** "the edges continue into each other so it can repeat". **Glows:** "glowing light on a solid pure black background".
- **Bigger than 2304 wide.** The existing scripts upscale only by the 1.5× second pass. So the `-hd` copies (4096 × 2048 planets, clouds and night maps; 8192 × 4096 skies) are made two ways:
  1. **Default:** the tidy script upscales the 2304-wide painting with Lanczos, a light sharpen and a little film grain. Clean and soft, fine for a texture on a ball. This is what to do tonight.
  2. **Better, if the PC has an upscale model:** `python3 scripts/trader-forge-art-gen.py --check` lists the PC's upscale models (4x-UltraSharp, RealESRGAN and so on). If there is one, run the painter with `--hd-model NAME` and it paints 4096 × 2048 and 8192 × 4096 directly. It is slower and the PNGs are big (a sky is about 60 MB), so test with one picture first (`--only skies/0 --hd-model NAME`).
- **Skies and seams.** Z-Image doesn't make left and right edges match. The tidy script blends the seam on every wide map (as it did for the existing planets). The sky's top and bottom are the poles of a sphere and stay as painted; stars there will look pinched, which hides in the dark.

## 5. Bump maps (height and normal maps)

The viewer wants a normal map beside every planet surface so mountains and craters catch the sunlight. Nobody paints these; they are made from the surface in two steps.

1. **Height map:** how high each spot is.
   - **If the PC has Depth Anything** (the `DepthAnythingV2Preprocessor` or `DepthAnythingPreprocessor` node from the `comfyui_controlnet_aux` pack; `--check` says), run the `heights` group (it is part of batch 1). It sends each planet picture to the depth model and saves `heights/<name>.png`. About 4 seconds each.
   - **If it doesn't,** do nothing. The tidy script makes the height map itself from the picture: its light and dark, softened a little so bright ridges stand up and dark craters sink. This is already a good bump for a rocky or desert world and is the default.
   - Honest note: a depth model reads a flat texture as a picture of a scene, so its answer is a rough guess. When it exists the tidy script uses 60% of it and 40% of the picture's own light and dark.
2. **Normal map:** the tidy script turns the height map into a normal map (the usual blue-purple picture) with the left and right edges wrapping, so a turning planet shows no line. Strength is set per kind: 2.0 for most, 0.25 for gas giants and 0.2 for ice giants (they have no ground to bump). The numbers are `NORMAL_STRENGTH` and `NORMAL_DEFAULT` at the top of `scripts/trader-forge-art.py`.
3. Saved as `planets/<kind>-<n>-normal.webp` (1024 × 512) and `-normal-hd.webp` (4096 × 2048).

## 6. Checks, the review page and remakes

**Child-safety checklist** (every picture, before it goes in; Year 4 to 6 pupils, 8 to 11 years old):
- Creatures are friendly and wondrous: big eyes, soft shapes, no teeth bared, no claws raised, nothing hunting, fighting or hurt.
- No blood, no bones, no dead or injured creatures, no weapons, no skulls.
- No people shown close up. If a tiny figure appears, it is fully dressed and gentle.
- No scary faces on planets or suns (planets sometimes come out looking like a face or an eye; the eyeball world is meant to).
- Robots look friendly (round, big lens eyes), not menacing or military.
- No letters, words, numbers, logos or signs.
- Nothing that looks like a real brand, flag or religious symbol.
- Dark pictures (night maps, the black hole, the darkest skies) stay pretty, not frightening.

**Quality checklist:**
- **Maps** (planets, moons, suns, clouds, night, skies): a flat map and not a ball; no stars or black space round the edge (except skies, clouds and night, which are meant to have black); no text; no single bright "sun spot" or dark corner (a left-to-right brightness change shows as a stripe as it turns); the contact sheet shows each map twice side by side, so the join in the middle must not show.
- **Clouds:** white on really black, with real gaps. A map that is mostly grey is remade. The "spot" map has exactly one big oval storm.
- **Rings:** clear vertical stripes with some black gaps. The "double" one has a wide black gap in the middle.
- **Skies:** stars sharp and small, no planet or bright sun in them.
- **Postcards:** the creature is the one named, in the right place, friendly; plants under the red sun look dark, under the blue sun look red.
- **Cut-outs:** the whole object, one object, plain white round it.
- **Buttons:** reads clearly at 100 pixels wide.

**Remake rule:** one try of each picture. Claude flags every bad one with the reason, and remakes it with `python3 scripts/trader-forge-art-gen.py --only planets/desert-2`. That keeps the old picture in `<group>/_old/` and paints a new one with a new seed. If a second try is still wrong, Claude changes the words and tries once more, then tells James.

**Review page:** after each batch, `scripts/trader-forge-art.py --sheets DIR` makes contact sheets of the inbox (maps shown twice to see the join). Claude looks at them, builds a review page of the whole batch as a private artifact (as for the Star Trader paintings), lists what it remade and why, and James looks at it. James can ask for any picture to be remade; each batch goes into the game only after the page is checked.

## 8. Things to decide or watch

- **Repo size.** The big copies are large. A real 4096 × 2048 planet is about 1 to 2 MB and an 8192 × 4096 sky about 3 MB, so the whole set could be about 150 MB (the tidy script prints the real total and how much of it is `-hd`). That is too much to commit lightly. Options for later: keep the normal-size files in git and host the `-hd` ones elsewhere (Supabase storage); paint only the best 2 surfaces per kind at 4096; or lower `HD_QUALITY` at the top of the tidy script (70 now). Decide after batch 1 when the real sizes are known. Nothing is committed until then.
- **Night maps: painted, then left out (4 Nov).** The model painted Earth's real continents on almost every one, so the lights would never match a planet's own land. The viewer already draws city lights, lava glow and crystal veins on each planet's own surface (`bakeShaders.ts`), so the painted night maps are not copied into the game. The paintings stay in the inbox; `nightArt()` finds nothing and the drawn lights are used.
- **Technology sizes, sun, moon and night sizes aren't in `types.ts`.** The tidy script's table `SIZE` at the top says what it writes; change a number and re-run with `--force`.
- **Cloud kinds:** `types.ts` says `clouds/<cloudKind>.webp`. "none" has no picture.
- **The ring world, cube and donut maps** are ordinary flat maps (the strange shape is the viewer's job). The eyeball map has the sunlit ocean or desert in the middle and ice at the left and right edges, so it joins up when wrapped; the viewer should put the middle of the map under the sun.

- **First-look remakes (4 Nov).** 20 of batch 1 were remade once: suns and gas giants that came out as a ball drawn inside the map (their prompts now say "close-up of ..." and never name a star or a planet), cloud maps with grey or tinted backgrounds (the tidy script now treats the darkest fifth of a cloud map as black), and a few planets with frames, a stamp-like pattern or a globe inside.
