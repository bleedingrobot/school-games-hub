# Basic facts training: plan

*A practice area in the hub, separate from the games, where students build their basic facts: learn a strategy first, then get quicker, with their own speed shown as colours on their page. Written 18 October 2026 for James, from his research document "Evidence-Based Design Architecture for a New Zealand Primary Basic Mathematics Facts Digital Practice System".*

**Status: on hold. Brainstormed with James on 18 October 2026, and he answered every question in section 12 the same day. Nothing is built. It waits until Clan Quest and Dragon Maths are finished, and it needs stage F.1 of [MATHS-FOCUS-PLAN.md](MATHS-FOCUS-PLAN.md) (the skill engine) first.**

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Training is its own place in the hub, next to the games but not part of them. Each day a student does a short session of facts chosen for them. New facts start with a strategy lesson and a picture (ten-frame, array, split rectangle, 10×10 grid), and only later does speed matter. The hub quietly times every answer, takes off how long that child takes just to type, and colours each fact on the student's page from grey-blue ("New to me") to bright green ("Just know it!"). There are no clocks on screen, no leaderboards and no lost progress. Training follows the same skill ladder as the maths focus plan, from facts to 10 up to percentages, with fractions, decimals and percentages starting from "these are the same" (0.5 = 50% = 5/10 = ½).

## 2. Ideas from the research we're following

1. **Strategy before speed.** A student learns a way to work a fact out (doubles, make ten, 7×8 = 5×8 + 2×8) before being asked to be quick. Working it out is a step, not a failure.
2. **Timing is hidden while answering.** No countdowns, ticking bombs or speed scores during a session. Times are only shown afterwards, on the student's own page, as colours.
3. **Mistakes are calm and teach.** A wrong answer shows the right one with a picture; the student types it and carries on. Progress is never wiped.
4. **Fact families.** 6×7, 7×6, 42÷6 and 42÷7 are one family, and so are ½, 0.5, 50% and 5/10.
5. **Small, mixed, spaced practice.** A few new facts mixed with known ones, short daily sessions, older facts brought back over days.
6. **Compete with yourself.** Rewards come from practising on different days and getting facts right, never from raw speed. No class rankings.

### What we're doing differently from the document

- **No fixed "under 1.5 seconds" rule.** Year 4s tapping on Chromebooks are slower typists, so each child's typing time comes off first (section 5).
- **No heavy maths model.** The document describes a Bayesian model. The Leitner boxes the hub already has, plus the speed colours, give the same "don't know / working it out / just know it" levels far more simply.
- **Separate from the games (James, 18 Oct 2026).** Training gives no bonuses in Clan Quest, Dragon Maths, Gem Keep or anything else, and the games don't give training rewards.

## 3. What a student sees

### Training home

- **Today's session** button (about 3 minutes).
- **My facts map** for the stage they're on (section 6).
- **This week:** one friendly line, for example "5 facts turned green this week!" Never "you got slower".
- **Strategy lessons** they've unlocked, to replay any time.
- **Quick-fire 20** (optional, section 4.5).

### A session

- About 3 minutes or 20–30 questions, whichever comes first.
- **The mix:** mostly facts they're learning now, some review of older facts (weakest and most overdue first), and a few easy known facts, with one at the end so every session finishes on a win. For a student who is struggling, the learning set shrinks to 2 new facts among about 4 known ones, as the document suggests.
- **No clock, no score out of 10.** At the end: how many they practised, and any facts that changed colour.
- **Big number pad** (works with a keyboard and the number keys too), with optional **auto-submit** once the right number of digits is typed, so slow Enter presses don't count against them.

## 4. Activities

### 4.1 Strategy lesson

Each new strategy gets a short lesson the first time its facts come up: a picture, a worked example, then two or three guided tries. Strategies by stage:

| Facts | Strategy | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Facts to 10, bonds to 10 | See it in a ten-frame | Ten-frame |
| Doubles and halves | Doubles, then halving as the reverse | Two matching rows |
| Facts to 20 | Make ten (8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3), near doubles (6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1) | Double ten-frame, rekenrek-style rows |
| ×2, ×5, ×10 | Doubling, counting in fives and tens, half of ×10 for ×5 | Arrays |
| ×3, ×4, ×8 | ×4 is double double, ×8 is double ×4 | Arrays split in halves |
| ×6, ×9 | ×6 is double ×3, ×9 is ×10 take one lot | Split rectangle |
| ×7 | ×5 plus ×2 | Split rectangle |
| ×11, ×12 | ×10 plus one or two lots | Split rectangle |
| Division | The reverse of times (42 ÷ 6: what times 6 makes 42?) | Fact family triangle |
| Fractions, decimals, percentages | The same amount three ways | 10×10 grid, fraction bar |

### 4.2 Split the rectangle

7×8 shows as a grid of squares. The student drags a line to cut it into two parts they know (5×8 and 2×8), types each part, then the total. This is the main activity for derived times tables in Years 4–6.

### 4.3 Which trick helps?

"Which helps with 6×8?" with three strategies to tap (double 3×8 / add 6 eight times / 6 + 8). This practises choosing a method, not remembering an answer.

### 4.4 Fact family triangle and missing number

- **Triangle:** three numbers (6, 7, 42), and the student makes all four facts.
- **Missing number:** ? × 7 = 42, 8 + ? = 15, ½ = ?/10.

### 4.5 Quick-fire 20

An optional 20-second round, only using facts the student already gets right. They try to beat their own count. It's offered to students who are accurate but slow, to nudge them from working it out to just knowing it. Nothing is lost by doing badly.

### 4.6 Fix-it moment

After a wrong answer, a picture shows why (the array, ten-frame or grid), the right answer is shown, and the student types it in. It doesn't count as a right answer for that fact. If the same kind of mistake keeps happening (section 7), the strategy lesson for that fact comes back.

### 4.7 Stretch

For students whose facts are green, training extends the same facts: 6×7 → 60×7 → 0.6×7, bonds to 100 and 1000, and two-step questions. These follow the ladder's later skills, so stretch is just "a step ahead on the ladder".

## 5. Speed colours (James, 18 Oct 2026)

### Thinking time, not typing time

- **Typing check.** At the start of a session (and the first time ever), the student types two or three numbers they can see, for example "42". That gives their typing time for a 1-digit and a 2-digit answer, and a tap time for multiple choice.
- **Thinking time = answer time − typing time** for an answer of that length. The typing time used is the middle of their last several checks, so one slow check doesn't change everything.
- Example: a child takes 1.2 s to type "42" and answers 6×7 in 2.5 s. Their thinking time is 1.3 s: bright green.

### The bands

| Thinking time | Colour | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Under 1.5 s | Bright happy green | Just know it! |
| 1.5–3.5 s | Light green | Quick thinking |
| 3.5–5 s | Yellow | Getting there |
| 5–8 s | Light orange | Working it out |
| 8–10 s | Soft blue | Still learning |
| 10 s or more | Grey-blue | New to me |

The 1.5 s and 3.5 s lines come from the research: under 1.5 s is remembering, 1.5–3.5 s is a quick strategy.

### Rules

- **Students see the colour and label only, never the seconds** (James, 18 Oct 2026).
- **Calm colours at the slow end, never red** (James, 18 Oct 2026), because red and speed pressure make children anxious about maths.
- **No colours or times during a session.** They show on the facts map and the end-of-session summary.
- **A fact's colour is the middle of its last 3 answers,** so one slow day doesn't knock it back. A wrong answer counts as "New to me" in that middle.
- **"Known"** for moving on means bright green on two different days. Light green counts as secure enough to keep going to the next fact set, so slower typers and thinkers aren't stuck.
- **All the numbers are in config,** so the bands can be changed without touching code.

### Too fast and wrong

If a student keeps answering very quickly and wrongly (guessing), the number pad stays locked for about a second after each question appears, so they have to look first. It switches off again once they're answering carefully.

## 6. My facts map

- **Times tables:** the 12×12 grid, each square in its speed colour. 6×7 and 7×6 colour together, and division facts show on the same squares.
- **Early facts:** a garden (James, 18 Oct 2026). Each fact is a plant that grows through the speed colours, from seed ("New to me") to full flower ("Just know it!"). Facts due for review droop a little but never die.
- **Fractions, decimals and percentages:** one tile per family (½ = 0.5 = 50% = 5/10).
- **Facts due for review** glow softly. They never turn red or lose their colour.
- Tapping a fact shows its family and its strategy lesson.

## 7. Spotting mistakes

Common mistake patterns get a name, so the hub (and later the teacher) can see them:

| Pattern | Example | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Added instead of times | 6×8 → 14 | Array lesson for that table |
| Off by one lot | 7×8 → 48 (did 6×8) | Split rectangle for that fact |
| Next-door fact | 7×8 → 54, 63 | More spaced review of that fact |
| Adding tops and bottoms | ½ + ¼ → 2/6 | Fraction bar picture |
| "Longer decimal is bigger" | picks 0.25 over 0.3 | 10×10 grid picture |
| Percent equals the digits | 0.25 → 25 (not 25%) | Grid showing 25 of 100 |

The wrong answers in multiple choice use the same patterns, as section 3 of the maths focus plan already says.

## 8. The order: the skill ladder

Training follows the ladder in section 12 of [MATHS-FOCUS-PLAN.md](MATHS-FOCUS-PLAN.md), the NZC teaching sequence (James, 18 Oct 2026), all the way up, not just to division. That means the times tables come in NZC families: ×2, ×5, ×10; then ×3, ×4, ×8; then ×6, ×9; then ×7; then ×11, ×12, each with its division facts.

Training adds a strategy lesson to each fact step (section 4.1) and uses the placement check from the maths focus plan, so a Year 6 student doesn't start on bonds to 10. **Students can't skip ahead** (James, 18 Oct 2026): they follow the ladder from where placement puts them, and stretch questions (section 4.7) keep fast students challenged.

### Fractions, decimals and percentages start with "the same as" (James, 18 Oct 2026)

Before fractions or percentages of amounts and adding decimals, students learn the equivalences, in this order:

1. **Halves:** ½ = 0.5 = 50% = 5/10
2. **Quarters:** ¼ = 0.25 = 25%, ¾ = 0.75 = 75%
3. **Tenths:** 1/10 = 0.1 = 10%, up to 9/10 = 0.9 = 90%
4. **Fifths:** 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%, 2/5 = 0.4 = 40%… (linked back to tenths)
5. **Hundredths:** 0.07 = 7% = 7/100, 0.35 = 35%
6. **Equivalent fractions:** 2/4 = ½, 4/8 = ½, 6/10 = 3/5
7. **Stretch:** eighths (1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%) and thirds (⅓ ≈ 33%)

Then fractions of amounts (½ of 18, ⅓ of 15), percentages of amounts (25% of 40) and adding decimals. **Fractions of a number wait until steps 1–3 are done** (James, 18 Oct 2026), even though the NZC ladder has ⅓ of 15 at Year 3, so training moves that step later than auto does.

- **One picture, three ways.** A 10×10 grid or fraction bar shades the amount, and the student sees that 0.5, 50% and ½ cover the same space.
- **Match-up questions:** "Which is the same as 0.25?" with three choices, using the multiple-choice text answers from stage F.6 of the maths focus plan.
- **Each equivalence family is one fact** with its own record and colour, like a times-table family.

## 10. Comfort and access

- Big buttons in easy thumb reach; full keyboard and number-key support.
- Auto-submit (optional, per student).
- Read-aloud for younger students, with te reo Māori numbers as an option later.
- Calm mode: no moving backgrounds or extra animation, high contrast, bigger text.

## 11. Saving

- **Training keeps its own records** (James, 18 Oct 2026), in its own table, separate from `record_fact_answers`. Training answers never change what the games ask, and game answers never change training.
- **Each answer records** the fact key (same key style as the maths focus plan: `mul:6x7`, `bond10:3+7`, and new ones such as `fdp:half`), right or wrong, answer time, typing time used, and the day.
- **Times are measured on the device** so slow school internet doesn't make answers look slow. Answers are saved in a small queue and sent when the connection is back, so a session doesn't break if the Wi-Fi drops.
- **Checking:** questions come from seeds in `shared/`, like the games, so the server can rebuild and mark them.

## 14. Training before games (James, 22 Oct 2026)

In a class with the switch on, a student does one Training session, of their choice, before any game opens. The games then stay open for 6 hours (`TRAINING_CONFIG.gate.gamesOpenHours`), and the next session opens them for another 6.

James's answers:

1. **What counts:** a whole "Today's session" (it can be short if the 3 minutes run out) or a whole Quick looks round (all 12 pictures). "Finish for now" early doesn't count.
2. **A switch per class** on the Teacher page ("Training before games"), off to start; James turns it on for his three classes. Teachers are never locked.
3. **The lock is on the website only.** Game pages, typed addresses and the Lobby show "Training first!" instead. No game's server function was changed, so a student who knew how to fiddle with the browser could get round it.
4. **A game already open carries on** when the time runs out, and so do classmate games already started (the home page's "still going" links). Only starting a new game waits.

How it works:

- `shared/training/gate.ts` decides (locked, or open until when). The finish time is the newer of the server's (`my_training_lock()`) and this device's own (so a session finished with the Wi-Fi down opens the games straight away).
- The class's switch comes from the settings row every page already reads, so a class with it off costs no extra request. With it on, `my_training_lock()` is asked at most once a minute. **If it can't be asked, the games stay open.**
- The training function saves a `session` item after the session's answers; `record_training_session()` only counts it when enough of its answers are saved (`gate.minAnswers`: 2 for facts, 12 for Quick looks) and it finished in the last 12 hours.
- Migration `20261022000300_training_first.sql`: `training_sessions`, `app_settings.training_first`, `teacher_set_training_first()`, `my_training_lock()`.
- To turn it off fast: the Teacher page switch, or `update public.app_settings set training_first = false;`

## 15. Ladder fixes (James, 22 Oct 2026)

An audit on 22 Oct found that nobody had got past step 1 (×1, ×2, ×5, ×10: 122 facts) and that the colours meant little. Four changes, all in the website and the shared Training code; nothing new is stored and the database is unchanged.

1. **An honest warm-up.** The typing check was slower than real answers (typical warm-up 2.6 s, typical answer 1.8 s), so 4 in 5 right answers came out as no thinking time and turned bright green; some students had the 10 s cap taken off every answer. Now the fastest of the last 5 checks is used, never more than a ceiling (1 s, 1.5 s, 2 s for 1, 2, 3 digits, `typing.ceilingMs`), a slip restarts the clock, and the very first check starts with an untimed practice number. The ceiling is applied when an answer is scored, so **old answers were scored again with it** (James's choice): on 22 Oct's records, "Just know it!" went from 92% of tried facts to 68%.
2. **Starting place.** Training now uses the maths warm-up (section 8 always said it would): every times-table step below the student's warm-up result or furthest games place is passed (`shared/training/start.ts`, `TrainingState.passedSteps`, worked out when Training opens). In a class with auto on, Training asks for the warm-up before the first session.
3. **Flying.** A student with 90% right and 75% at "Quick thinking" or faster over their last 24 answers learns 12 new facts per session instead of 6 (`session.flying`). On 22 Oct's records 26 students would have been flying.
4. **Practice fortnight (James).** When a student starts struggling (under 60% right over their last 12 answers), Training stops pushing them on: for 14 days there are no new facts, and every session is facts they have already met, best known first (`rest`, `isResting`). It starts on the day they begin struggling and isn't stretched while it runs; if they are still struggling afterwards, the next wrong-heavy run starts another. Their Training home says so kindly. A student with no facts yet carries on as usual. Worked out from the saved answers like everything else. On 22 Oct's records 2 students would have been in one.

The `training` server function only bundles the settings file, so redeploying it changes nothing it does; it is redeployed to keep its "up to date" test honest.
