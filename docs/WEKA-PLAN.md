# Weka's Stepping Stones (making ten to add within 20) — plan

Short id `weka`. **Status: built 4 Nov 2026 with clay stand-in art and Bella's voice; final paintings in
`docs/WEKA-ART-PLAN.md`.** Game D in `docs/EARLY-GAMES-PLAN.md`; the parts every early game shares are in section 7 there
and aren't repeated here. Built alongside Tūī's Ten Tree (`docs/TUI-PLAN.md`), with the same look, voice, words and way
of fading help.

## 1. The game in plain words

Weka, a cheeky brown bush bird that can't fly, wants to cross a stream on stepping stones to reach a picnic. The stones
are numbered and come in fives (light, then dark); stone 10 is one big flat rock.

- **Today's challenge.** "8 plus 5. Weka is on 8 and hops 5 more. Where will Weka land?" While a fact is new, it comes
  in two steps: first "How many hops to the big rock?" (2), Weka hops to 10, Bella says "8 and 2 make 10. Then 3 more.",
  then the child taps where Weka lands (13). Once the fact is secure it is one tap with a fainter picture; once it is
  known well it is just "8 + 5 = ?". A wrong number wobbles back, no penalty. Ten goes.
- **The magic things.** 10 big stones on the far bank, bronze, silver then gold, one per day played.
- **The win.** The Golden Picnic Basket (weka are famous for pinching picnics). Then a new friend's stream: Kiwi,
  Takahē, Kākāpō, Pūkeko, Kea.

## 2. What the research says about teaching making ten

1. **Making ten rests on three smaller facts** (NCETM Year 2 spine 1.11; Oak National Academy, "Add and subtract two
   numbers that bridge through 10"): what makes 10 with the first number (8 and **2**), splitting the second number
   (5 is **2** and **3**), and ten and some more (10 and 3 is **13**). A wrong answer usually means one of the three broke,
   so the help shows all three separately before it joins them.
2. **Start with 9, then 8, then 7 and 6** (NCETM; Murata 2004 and Murata and Fuson 2006 on Japanese Grade 1 "make-a-ten"):
   9 + n needs one hop to ten, the easiest split. The levels follow this order.
3. **Use a picture with the ten structure in it** (ten-frames, Rekenrek, a number line in fives). Murata found children
   move from structured objects, to drawing the split, to doing it in their heads; the stones are a number line coloured in
   fives with ten as a stand-out rock, so "how far to ten" is visible.
4. **The number line is a strong picture for young children** (Siegler and Ramani 2008: short, straight, numbered board
   games noticeably improve number sense). The stepping stones are one straight line.
5. **Start on the bigger number** (commutativity). From day 11, some goes put the smaller number first (5 + 8) and the
   help says "Start on the bigger number."
6. **Taking away across ten mirrors it** (NCETM 1.11: "subtract through ten"): 13 − 5 is back 3 to 10, then back 2.
   Gold days end with two hop-backs (James, 4 Nov 2026).
7. **Keep supports until the child can do it, then remove them** (Oak: "once a pupil can confidently visualise making
   10 ... remove the ten frame"; Kalyuga's expertise reversal: help that a child no longer needs slows them down). So the
   help fades fact by fact, from the child's own record, and comes back if they slip.
8. **Say it the same way every time** (NCETM stem sentences): "8 and 2 make 10. Then 3 more." Tūī's Ten Tree uses the
   same words ("ten and four is fourteen"), and both games say the te reo number after a right answer ("Tekau mā toru").

## 3. The maths

- **Skill:** adding within 20 by making ten. NZ Year 2 (`MS.Y2.NUM.OP.P1`), ladder skill `facts-20`.
- **No new shared maths.** The shared `add:a+b` keys (a ≤ b) with both numbers 9 or less and a total of 11 to 18: 20
  facts. The shared pack makes every question, its wording and its wrong choices. Variants used: 1 "8 + 5" (bigger
  first), 0 "5 + 8" (smaller first, from silver days, about 3 goes in 10) and 2 "13 − 5" (hop back, the last 2 goes of
  gold days). All three record against the same fact, as anywhere on the hub.
- **Levels** (from the child's records, 70% of a level secure moves them on, never back): 1 starting on 9 (8 facts),
  2 starting on 8 (6), 3 starting on 7 and 6 (6), 4 all mixed (20). From level 2, up to 3 goes review earlier levels.
- **The first step ("how many hops to the big rock?") is help only**: it is never sent to the server or recorded.

## 4. Help that fades (the scaffolds)

Help follows the child's results, not the days (the same choice James made for Tūī, 4 Nov 2026). Each go carries its
help level from the server, worked out from the child's record of that fact:

| The child's record of the fact | Help | What they see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New or learning (box 0 or 1) | **Full** | every stone numbered; the hops shown as two colours (orange to the big rock, blue after); two steps: hops to 10, then where Weka lands; Bella says the make-ten sentence |
| Secure (box 2) | **Faint** | only 5, 10, 15, 20 numbered, the big rock stands out; the hops in one colour; one tap |
| Known well (box 3 or 4) | **None** | just "8 + 5 = ?" and the water |

A wrong answer drops a fact to box 1, so its full help returns next time. Inside a go, hints build:

1. First wrong tap: a wobble, "Try again!"
2. Second: the picture goes up a level, the stones to the big rock light up and Bella says the make-ten sentence (on the
   first step, Bella counts the hops to the big rock with the child instead).
3. Third: the hand points to the right number.

Also: after three goes in a row with a wrong first tap, full help comes back for the rest of the round; "Hear it again"
reads the question again; after a right answer with help, Bella says the te reo number and the sentence ("Tekau mā toru.
10 and 3 make 13."). After every right answer the child fills in **the split they used** as a part-whole bond (James, 5 Nov 2026): the
smaller number on top, then two empty circles in the hop colours, orange for the hops to the big rock and blue for the hops
after it. They tap a number for each, and Bella says it in full: "You solved 9 + 5 by splitting the 5 into 1 and 4. You
added the 1 first to make ten, then the other 4." This makes the hidden step of making ten (NCETM's "partition the second
number") something the child says back every go, the way part-whole models are used in Pipi's Hidden Shells. It is help
only and never recorded; after two wrong taps the hand points. Teachers can set **Picture help** to Fades (above), Always on, or Off, and see each child's hardest
facts.

On narrow phone screens the stones sit in two rows of ten (1 to 10 ending at the big rock, then 11 to 20), like a
twenty-frame; on wider screens they are one line, 5 to 20.

## 6. Guided first go

The very first question is always 9 + 2 with full help. The hand points at 1 (one hop to the big rock), then at 11.

One per Golden Picnic Basket, per friend. No points.
