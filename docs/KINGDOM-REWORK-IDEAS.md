# Kingdom Keeper: why kids don't like it, and what to do

*6 Nov 2026. James: "kids don't like it, find it unfun and confusing. Research, brainstorm, up to a complete rewrite." Sources: the live database (read-only), a play-through of the preview, and outside research.*

## 1. What the numbers say (live, students only)

| | |
|---|---|
| Reigns started | 17, by 16 pupils (out of 156 on the hub) |
| Finished a reign | **0** (furthest anyone got: year 4 of 10) |
| Never got past year 1 | 12 of 17. Seven of those never answered a single council question |
| Played a second reign | 1 pupil |

| Reigns since 28 Oct | 2. Nobody in the last week |
| The big one | **3 of the 5 reigns that reached year 2 fed nobody in year 1.** 95–100% of the people left, and the city dropped from 100 to about 10. Those cities still had 2,300–3,150 bushels unspent in the granary |

Because nobody finishes, nobody has ever earned a point, a rating or an achievement from it. Sky Isles, Archery and Diamond Drop each had 21–24 players last week.

## 2. Why: what a kid meets

I played year 1 as a pupil would:

1. **A wall of numbers first.** The opening is a seven-line ledger (people, acres, bushels an acre, rats, grain, land price) before anything happens.
2. **Three quiz questions as a toll gate.** The maths is a pop quiz *before* the game, and the prizes (a 1.5× newcomer rate, 2 off the land price) are abstract. Most kids skip it.
3. **Four strange quantities and three conversion rules at once:** bushels, acres, people, price; 20 bushels a person, 2 acres a bushel, 10 acres a farmer. That's too many for 8–11 year olds to juggle. The research on this says keep 2–3 live quantities and add one new one at a time.
4. **Feeding starts at 0, and you can walk straight past it.** The planner has four tabs. A kid who clicks through without typing feeds nobody, and loses 90% of the city in one move. That is exactly what the data shows. The warning comes *after* the damage, and nobody recovered.
5. **Nothing to see change.** The pictures are lovely but fixed. The city doesn't visibly grow, fields don't fill, people don't arrive. Progress is a number in a bar.
6. **The decisions are either a trap or trivial.** Once you know the trick (Feed everyone, Plant all you can, keep some spare), every year is the same three clicks, ten times over. Before you know it, you lose. There's no middle where it's interesting.
7. **The bad things feel random.** Rats and sickness are dice rolls you can't see coming or prevent, except through a quiz question you skipped.
8. **Too long for the pay-off.** Ten years is about 60 screens, and the points only come at the very end.
9. **Small bug:** the question pad didn't take keyboard typing in the preview (tapping works). Worth checking it isn't hub-wide.

Hamurabi (1968) was a teletype game. Its fun was in the reader imagining the city. Kids today expect to *see* it.

## 3. What fun games for this age do (research summary)

- **See it change right away** (Townscaper, Kingdom Two Crowns, Islanders): every tap makes something visible appear.
- **One decision at a time with a clear stake** (Reigns: swipe a card, see four meters move).
- **Forecast before you choose** (Lemonade Stand: the weather is shown, so a bad day is your call, not bad luck).
- **Start tiny, add one thing a round** (Mini Metro).
- **Put the maths inside the action, not before it.** In Habgood & Ainsworth's *Zombie Division* study (ages 7–11), maths that *was* the game mechanic taught more than the same maths given as a quiz between turns. Kingdom Keeper's council is the quiz-between-turns kind.

Overlaps to avoid in the hub: building a village (Clan Quest), buying low and selling high (Star Trader).

## 4. Options, smallest to biggest

### A. First aid (half a day, ship this week)

Stops the instant collapse; doesn't make it fun.
- Feeding starts at "everyone" and planting at "all you can, keeping some spare". Kids then only change things on purpose.
- A turn with fewer than half fed asks "Are you sure? 95 people will leave" before it goes.
- Shorten the reign to **5 years**, give points for each year survived (not just the end), and cut the opening ledger to three lines with icons.
- Fix the keyboard on the question pad.

### B. Rework: same game, made visible (about 3–5 days) ⭐ recommended

Keep Riverstone, the art, the server, the advisers. Change the whole middle.
- **One screen a year, built on a picture of the valley.** The fields are a grid of plots you tap to plant, and they fill with crops. Little people stand by the granary, and fed ones turn happy. Houses appear as the city grows (the city-1…4 pictures already exist).
- **Two numbers only: grain and people.** Land is shown as plots, not acres. Drop the land market completely (it's the most confusing part, and Star Trader already does trading).
- **The maths *is* the move (intrinsic).** To feed: "24 people × 5 sacks = ?" and your answer is what goes out. Get it wrong and the steward shows the right amount on a bar model, so you're never punished for a wrong answer. To plant: "6 rows of 8 plots = ?". Questions still come from each pupil's maths focus where they fit, and the advisers' questions become optional "bonus" asks.
- **Forecast cards instead of hidden dice.** "Nettle hears rats in the walls this year", so a kid can choose to guard the granary (costs grain) or risk it.
- **5 short years (about 6–8 minutes), a star rating each year, and the city picture grows in front of you.**

### C. Complete rewrite: "Royal Decree" (about 1–2 weeks)

A different game in the same world, so new it needs a new name.
- **Reigns-style cards.** Each card is a painted villager with a request that has a number in it: "The baker needs 6 trays of 8 loaves for the feast. We have 50. Enough?" You swipe yes/no, or answer the number. Four meters (Food, Gold, Happiness, Safety) wobble. Survive as long as you can.
- Each reign is 2–4 minutes, so it's very replayable. It's funny, character-driven, and uses the advisers and art we already have. The maths is in every card.
- Risk: the 6 advisers + 8 rulers aren't enough faces. We'd need about 20–30 villager portraits painted on the gaming PC.

### D. Retire it

Switch it off and spend the time on something else. It's honest, given 0 completions. But the art and server are good, and B reuses nearly all of it.

## 5. Recommendation

Ship **A** now (cheap, stops kids bouncing off on turn 1), then build **B**. If B still doesn't land, C is the bolder swing.
