## 1. One-page summary

**What was reviewed.** The seven Foundational games for ages 5 to 8: Kiwi's Beanstalk (bonds to 10, 100, 1000), Hop's Glow-worm Pond (skip counting), Penguin Pairs (doubles and halves), Pipi's Hidden Shells (part-whole), Tūī's Ten Tree (teen numbers), Weka's Stepping Stones (make ten to add) and Quick looks (subitising). Each was scored 1 to 4 on eight things: maths fidelity, clarity, support, fading, progression, feedback, progress recording and engagement. The rubric comes from a research review of how to design maths games for young children. I also checked every game in the hub against a list of fundamental maths skills, to find the biggest gaps for new games.

**The headline.** The games are good. Totals run from 19 to 24 out of 32. Every game scores 3 for clarity: voice read-aloud, one question at a time, big buttons, calm pacing. The pictures are real maths models (ten-frames, number lines, pebbles, mirror fish, flashing dot pictures) and the answers are checked on the server. Weka's Stepping Stones is the best (24), then Quick looks (23) and Tūī (22). Beanstalk and Hop are lowest (19), mostly because the game asks one narrow question and the answer can be found without the maths.

**The shared weaknesses.**
1. **Mistakes teach nothing (all 7).** A wrong tap gets a wobble and "Try again!". Wrong taps are free, so tapping every choice always works. The wrong choices are mostly the answer plus or minus one, or easy to rule out by the last digit. They are not real misconceptions.
2. **Help fades by the calendar, not by the child (Beanstalk, Hop, Penguin Pairs, Pipi).** Bronze, silver and gold days are set by days played. Help never comes back for a child who is struggling. Tūī adapts, but a bug cancels the fading for a strong child.
3. **Careful children can be blocked.** A fact only moves up when it is right within 5 seconds. In Hop the clock starts while the voice is still reading the question.
4. **Teachers can't see who is stuck (all 7).** Per-fact data is saved, but there are no Today-page alerts and no class grid. Quick looks has no teacher view at all.
5. Also: ladders are short with no "what next" link between games, and guessing wins.

**Top 5 actions** (details in section 8):
1. **R1** Fix Tūī's fading bug (small job, high value).
2. **R2** Fair step-up for careful children: a junior time limit, and start the clock after the voice (small job, high value).
3. **R3 and R4** Quick wins in Quick looks and Beanstalk (small jobs).
4. **R5** Mistakes that teach: wrong choices built from real misconceptions, "show why" with the same picture, and a guided redo after too many free taps (large job, very high value).
5. **R6 and R7** Help that follows the child, and a teacher view of stuck pupils.

**Biggest gaps for new games** (ranked, section 7): (1) comparing, ordering and the number line; (2) equals as balance and missing numbers; (3) place value to 100 and beyond; (4) equal groups and arrays; (5) counting out a set; (6) money (sketch only).

**Live data (7 Nov).** Children are getting 80 to 95 percent of early facts right, so the games are not too hard. The risk is too easy, or answers found by elimination. The games are only 1 to 6 days old, so numbers are small. Re-run in 3 to 4 weeks.

**The rubric in short.** 1 = missing or harmful, 2 = present but weak, 3 = good, 4 = excellent and matches the research.
1. **Maths fidelity:** teaches the idea correctly, and winning needs the maths (not guessing or tapping everything).
2. **Clarity:** a 5 to 7 year old can start alone (voice, pictures, one thing at a time, big targets, no timer pressure).
3. **Support and structure:** models and help appear at the right moments, mostly automatically, with an example for new skills.
4. **Fading:** support steps back as the child succeeds and comes back if they struggle (4 needs both, per skill).
5. **Progression:** research order, adapts per child toward 75 to 90 percent success, needs steady success to move up, old skills come back.
6. **Feedback:** after a mistake the child sees what went wrong and why, using the same picture. Right or wrong only scores 2. No feedback or harmful feedback scores 1.
7. **Progress recording:** per-skill, per-attempt data saved (3), plus wrong answers chosen, help used and a clear teacher view of stuck children (4).
8. **Engagement:** fun comes from the maths itself, with real choice and visible growth.

## 3. Research summary

Full notes with sources: `docs/reviews/maths-review/research-maths.md` and `research-design.md`. Source numbers like [S8] point to the list in `research-design.md`.

### 3.1 The maths ideas (about half a page)

Eight ideas sit under the seven games. For each, real understanding means the child can explain it, not just answer fast.

- **Subitising.** Seeing "how many" without counting. Perceptual (1 to 4, instant) then conceptual (seeing 7 as 5 and 2, using 5 and 10 as anchors). A child who only knows the dice-5 picture, or who counts fast, has not got it. Years 0 to 2 mostly.
- **Counting and cardinality.** Stable order, one word per object, and the last word counted tells how many. "Give me 6" and stop at 6 is the real test. Years 0 to 1.
- **Part-whole and hidden amounts.** 7 is 3 and 4, 2 and 5, 6 and 1, and the whole stays the same. Years 1 to 3. (Pipi.)
- **Bonds to 10, then 100 and 1000.** Knowing pairs without counting, as a set of related facts. 30 + 70 = 100 is "3 tens and 7 tens make 10 tens". Years 1 to 2, then 3 to 5. (Beanstalk.)
- **Making ten to add.** 8 + 5: split the 5 into 2 and 3, fill up to 10, then 10 + 3. A real understanding also does 28 + 5. Years 2 to 3. (Weka.)
- **Teen numbers.** 13 is one ten and three ones. The English number words hide this (thirteen, fourteen sound back to front), which is why children write 31 for 13. Years 1 to 2. (Tūī.)
- **Doubles and halves.** A double is two equal groups, a half is one of two equal groups, and they are opposites. Doubles become tools (near doubles). Years 1 to 3. (Penguin Pairs.)
- **Skip counting in 2s, 5s, 10s.** Counting in equal jumps where each jump is a group size. A child who can only chant from the start cannot say what comes after 14 in twos. Years 1 to 3. (Hop.)

The research map also lists where the typical gaps are: equals as balance, comparing and ordering, place value beyond the teens, money, simple graphs. Seven design rules came out of the maths research: the idea must be needed to win; show a picture then fade it; feedback re-shows the maths; ask "how did you know?"; fluency after understanding; link facts to strategies; record evidence per idea.

Caveat: the 2025 curriculum pages on Tāhūrangi would not load for the research tools, so year placements are "typical" ones and should be checked against the official pages.

### 3.2 Game-design principles (about half a page)

These produced the 25-item checklist and the rubric.

1. **Intrinsic integration.** Children learn more, and choose to play longer, when the maths is the way you play, not a quiz between levels. Habgood and Ainsworth 2011 [S1].
2. **Cognitive load.** Put labels next to what they describe. Spoken words plus a picture beat lots of text. Pre-readers need a replay button. Sweller 1988 [S2]; Mayer and Moreno 2003 [S3].
3. **Scaffolding and fading.** Give help automatically when a child struggles, take it away when they succeed, and show a worked example first. Wood, Bruner and Ross 1976 [S4]; Wood and Wood 1996 [S5]; Renkl and Atkinson 2003 [S6]. Children who already know the skill are slowed down by help they don't need (Kalyuga et al. 2003 [S7]).
4. **Feedback.** "Right or wrong" is weak. A reason or hint, using the same picture, is better, and works best about the task and strategy, not about being clever. Hattie and Timperley 2007 [S8]; Shute 2008 [S9]; Mueller and Dweck 1998 [S10].
5. **Spacing and mixing.** Practice over several days beats cramming, and mixed question types help children choose methods. Cepeda et al. 2006 [S11]; Roediger and Karpicke 2006 [S12]; Rohrer and Taylor 2007 [S13].
6. **Mastery and adapting.** Move up on steady success, not one lucky answer; aim for about 75 to 90 percent right. Corbett and Anderson 1995 [S14]; Wilson et al. 2019 [S15].
7. **Motivation.** Choice, a sense of getting better and a connection to characters; rewards for just turning up can reduce interest. Ryan and Deci 2000 [S16]; Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999 [S17].
8. **Ages 5 to 8.** Voice, icons, big targets (aim 64 pixels or more), sessions of 5 to 10 minutes. Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015 [S18].
9. **What to record.** Per child and skill: attempts, right, the wrong answer chosen, level, help used, date, plus a view that flags stuck children. [S14]
10. **Evidence for apps.** Promising but "emerging" (Griffith et al. 2020 [S19]; Outhwaite et al. 2017 [S20]).

## 4. The seven games

Each section: how it works, strengths, gaps, each score with its main evidence (file:line), and bugs seen while playing. Full detail is in `docs/reviews/maths-review/games/<game>.md`.

### 4.1 Weka's Stepping Stones (24/32)

Folder `src/games/weka`, `shared/weka`. Played in the browser on 7 Nov: day 1 and 2 in full, the start of day 3, and the start of a gold day. The last two goes of a gold day (hop-backs) were read from the code.

**How it works.** Weka the bird stands on stepping stones 5 to 20 with a big rock at 10. The child gets a sum that crosses ten ("9 + 7"). With full help they tap "how many hops to 10?", Weka hops to the rock, the make-ten sentence appears ("9 + 1 = 10, then + 6"), then they tap where Weka lands. After that come pebble ten-frames and a part-whole bond. Ten goes a day, one stone a day turns bronze, silver, gold over 30 days. Help fades per fact: full, then split-first, then faint, then numbers only.

**Strengths.** The right strategy (make ten) with the right models (number line with a rock at 10, pebbles, bond), in the research order (9, 8, 7 and 6, then mixed). Support builds gently after wrong taps and never speaks the answer. Fading is real, per fact, in four steps and comes back after a slip; teachers can force help on or off. Missed days cost nothing.

**Gaps.** Wrong choices are answer plus or minus 1 or 2, not built around real errors (stopping at the rock, counting on from the wrong number). Tapping four choices costs nothing and the hand points after 3 wrong taps. The pebble and bond steps are done for the child. Level 1 is repetitive. Silver and gold days follow the calendar. Hop-backs (subtraction across ten) are only 2 goes on gold days. No Today alert, no class grid, no round summary, rounds deleted after 30 days.

**Scores.**
- **Fidelity 3.** Teaches make-ten with research-aligned models (`Goes.tsx:4-6`, `Goes.tsx:49-52`). Held back because choices are always within 2 of the answer and taps are free; level 1 step 1 is always "1". The pebble step stops at 10 so the child does not choose how many to move (`Pebbles.tsx:66-76`).
- **Clarity 3.** Everything read aloud with "Hear it again" and a pointing hand (`Goes.tsx:151-152, 310`). Three screens per go (hops, pebbles, bond) is a lot; answer stones partly cut off at 800x600.
- **Support 4.** Number line, coloured hops, make-ten sentence, counting together after 2 wrong taps, hand after 3 (`Goes.tsx:157-168, 182-187, 413-479`). Help never speaks the answer (`voiceLines.ts` header).
- **Fading 3.** Four steps per fact (`round.ts:101-108`), help returns after a wrong answer and after 3 struggling goes (`Goes.tsx:47`). Held back: very slow (about 12 days to "none"), the 5 second quick rule (`leitner.ts`, `config.ts:53`), and the clock only starts after the hop-to-ten step (`Goes.tsx:204`).
- **Progression 3.** Research order, adaptive by the 70 percent rule (`round.ts:92-96`), weakest facts first (`round.ts:127-134`). Gaps: level 1 is 8 facts for 10 goes; day type is by calendar, so a struggling child still gets hop-backs on day 21.
- **Feedback 2.** Good ladder of hints and a good right-answer line ("13! 10 and 3 make 13."), but the hints are the same whatever the child chose and nothing says why 11 is wrong (`words.ts`). No end-of-round summary.
- **Recording 3.** Every round, tap and time saved (`weka_rounds`), per-fact boxes in `gem_keep_facts`; the teacher's pupil page shows days, level and 3 hardest facts. Nothing on Today (no reference outside Weka files and the teacher page). Hints used are not saved.
- **Engagement 3.** Warm clay art, hops are the answer. Each go takes about 7 seconds in three parts; the reward (a stone turning) is once a day and abstract.

**Seen while playing.** Level 1 step 1 is always 1, so it is guessable. Choice sets vary and sometimes leave out the nearest numbers (`9+9` showed 15, 14, 18, 16). With 8 facts in 10 goes, two facts repeat; repeats don't count for the records (`play.ts:41-45`). The first go is fully told (hand plus stem). Hop animations stall in a hidden browser (preview only).

### 4.2 Quick looks, subitising (23/32)

Not a game folder. It is a Training activity at `/training?quick=1`, from the dashboard card (`src/pages/Dashboard.tsx:244-258`). Code in `shared/subitising`, `src/subitising`. Played in the preview; the flash is too fast for a tool to catch, so pictures, buttons and feedback were checked by script and screenshot.

**How it works.** A 6-picture starting check places the child on one of 7 stages. Each round is 12 pictures: "Get ready", a picture flashes for 0.6 to 1.2 seconds, five answer buttons stay locked for 1 second, the child taps how many. The picture then comes back with the groups marked ("It was 7: 5 and 2"). After two wrong in a row, or a long think, the next picture stays on screen. Seven plants in a garden grow as stages are finished. No clock, no score, no red.

**Strengths.** Real subitising design: the picture goes, the buttons are locked, groups are shown afterwards. Progress is rebuilt from saved answers so it can't drift. A stage needs every picture type seen, not just a good score. Good help and automatic step-back. Calm, fully voiced.

**Gaps.** Teachers can't see any of it. The "keep the picture" mode counts as full progress and can't be told apart in the data. Reaction time is never saved. The check is short and starts at stage 4. Single-group feedback teaches nothing. Nothing after stage 7.

**Scores.**
- **Fidelity 3.** Picture removed, answer lock 1 second (`config.ts:84`, `FlashRunner.tsx:84-91`); a 5-button guess is 20 percent so mastery can't be gamed (`engine.ts:46-54`). Held back: stage 1 barely needs subitising, stage 3 has only 4 and 5 across three picture types, and stage 6 (empty squares) is ten-frame complement, a different skill.
- **Clarity 3.** One question per screen, a Kokoro clip for every line, the answer never spoken before the child answers (`FlashRunner.tsx:158`). No demo before the first picture; buttons are visible but greyed during the flash.
- **Support 3.** Groups shown after a struggle, one "peek again" (`FlashRunner.tsx:158-172`). The help line for a single dot is wrong ("smaller groups?"), and gap help uses the wrong prompt (`FlashRunner.tsx:237`).
- **Fading 3.** Flash gets shorter as a stage goes well (`engine.ts:100-105`), help only after a struggle, step-back, 20 percent review. But guided mode is a manual switch.
- **Progression 3.** Seven research-ordered stages, binary-search placement (`placement.ts:34-65`), and mastery needs picture-type coverage as well as accuracy (`engine.ts:46-54`). Check is only 6 pictures; stage 3 to 4 is steep; nothing after stage 7 (`SubitisingApp.tsx:167`).
- **Feedback 3.** Re-shows the groups after every answer ("It was 7: 5 and 2", `voiceLines.ts:21-27`). That is the best part. Weak for single-group pictures ("It was 4."), and a right and wrong gap answer get the same sentence.
- **Recording 2.** Every answer saved to `training_answers` and enough to rebuild the stage, but no teacher screen reads it. `teacher_training_student()` exists (`supabase/migrations/20261018002600_clans_schools_and_classes.sql:875`) but nothing calls it. `ms` is always 0 (`saving.ts:15-21`).
- **Engagement 3.** The garden and ladybird are warm, and the maths is the whole activity. A short, same-shaped loop, and a child placed high sees a mostly full garden.

**Seen while playing.**
1. The check opens on stage 4 (3 to 7 dots), harsh for a 5 year old.
2. Answer buttons are greyed during "Get ready"; pre-readers will try to tap them.
3. A single-dot help picture says "Can you see smaller groups?". Gap help says the same instead of "How many empty squares?" (`FlashRunner.tsx:237`).
4. A right and a wrong gap answer get the same sentence.
5. Two lucky guesses in the 2-picture check move a child up a stage (4 percent chance a test).
6. Single-group pictures give no split in feedback.
7. Review pictures in stage 6 still use the "How many did you see?" heading.
8. The "keep the picture" mode still counts to pass a stage (`FlashRunner.tsx:98`, no guided flag in `engine.ts`). A child can pass every stage by counting. (Plan R3.)

### 4.3 Tūī's Ten Tree (22/32)

Folder `src/games/tui`, `shared/tui`. Played in the preview as a new child, with deliberate wrong taps, two perfect days, then jumped to other steps and help levels.

**How it works.** Ten goes a day. A full ten and some loose berries on two ten-frames: "10 and 4". The child taps the right nest (3 or 4 nests). A right answer: berries fly in, Bella says "14! 10 and 4 is 14", and arrow cards slide the 4 onto the 0 of the 10. A wrong tap wobbles and brings more picture back. Two things adapt separately: the step (which question types, 5 steps from "10 + n" to all mixed) and the help level (6 levels, from every berry counted in to the sum only). One flower a day over 30 days wins a Golden Nest.

**Strengths.** Strong research base and built to it: the ten shown first, the 41-for-14 reversal as a distractor, kete as one unit, arrow cards. The best support set of the early games. Answers are never shown before a right tap; the server replays taps. A rich record of every tap and its time.

**Gaps.** Fading is cancelled by the step change for a good child. Only nine facts (11 to 19) for the whole term. Wrong answers get only "Try again" plus more picture; the reversal is never named. Three nests at top levels is guessable. Nothing on the teacher Today page; no "stuck" signal. A 3.4 second pause after every right answer.

**Scores.**
- **Fidelity 3.** Ten first, "ten and four" in the voice, 41-for-14 reversal (`round.ts:225`), arrow cards (`Goes.tsx:242-253`). Held back: at the top levels the answer is the only teen-looking nest in many goes (14/20/41, 18/20/81), so "the one starting with 1" often works. Only 3 nests (`nestsFor`, `round.ts:209`).
- **Clarity 3.** Question read aloud each go (`Goes.tsx:83`), "Hear it again" always visible (`Goes.tsx:153`), a guided first go. But "Hear it again" reads the sum line, not the story line shown at level 5; the pointing hand covers berries on the guided go.
- **Support 4.** Six picture levels from one ten-frame, a Peek button, arrow cards at every level. After a wrong tap the picture rises one level, after a second it goes to level 5 and Bella tells the story (`round.ts:244-264`, `Goes.tsx:128`). Teacher can set Fades, Always, Off.
- **Fading 2.** Lovely on paper, cancelled in practice. A perfect child gets two good challenges at step 1, which fades help 5 to 4 (`round.ts:92-95`) and passes the step, adding 2 levels back (`round.ts:99-104`). The saved state after two perfect days read step 2, level 5. This repeats at every step change, so a strong child sees top-level help for about 8 good days. (Plan R1.) The 7-of-9 start (step 3, level 4) helps only if the maths records are there.
- **Progression 2.** Sensible order, but only adapts at day level (two good days to move), one number range throughout, no spiral into 10 + 10 or tens and ones, and no link to Weka. Within a day nothing adapts.
- **Feedback 2.** Excellent after a right answer. After a wrong one: a wobble, "Try again!" (`Goes.tsx:128`, `words.ts` tryAgain). The 41 reversal, the one wrong answer the research says to name ("that is four tens and one"), is never named. The third-wrong-tap fade can never run at levels 5 and 4 (only two wrong nests, `round.ts:259-262`).
- **Recording 3.** Step, level, days, every round and tap with time, one record per fact; the server replays taps (`shared/tui/play.ts:31-58`, `server.ts:132-170`). Teacher page shows step, level, last played and 3 hardest facts. Nothing on Today or Hardest. Fact keys are `tenand:n`, not the way, so "can do 10 + 4 but not 14 - 10" can't be seen.
- **Engagement 3.** Clay art, kete and berries are satisfying, flowers to a Golden Nest give a reason to return. Each right answer holds the screen 3.4 seconds (`Goes.tsx:24`), so ten goes include about 45 seconds of waiting.

**Seen while playing.** (1) A perfect child stays at help level 5 through steps 1 to 4. (2) Guessing: 3 nests, so tapping by shape or position often works; three picks cover every go. (3) A right answer after a wrong first tap gets the same praise and arrow cards, and is recorded as wrong. (4) "Hear it again" says a different line from the one spoken at level 5. (5) In "take the ten away" goes the picture still shows all 14 berries. (6) The weakest fact appears twice in a round. (7) Preview only: the tools panel covers the top-left. (8) The home page shows "Day 3 of 30" while days done is 2, harmless.

### 4.4 Pipi's Hidden Shells (21/32)

Folder `src/games/pipi`, `shared/pipi`. Played in the preview on days 1 to 6, then jumped to silver (day 10) and gold (day 20).

## 5. Patterns across games

1. **Mistakes teach nothing (all 7).** The biggest shared gap. A wrong tap gets a wobble and "Try again!". Wrong choices are mostly the answer plus or minus one, or easy to rule out by shape (only even, only ends in 0, only teen-shaped), not real misconceptions. Free wrong taps mean tapping every choice always wins.
2. **Help fades by the calendar, not the child (Beanstalk, Hop, Penguin Pairs, Pipi).** Bronze, silver and gold are set by days played. Help never returns for a struggling child. Tūī adapts but its bug cancels fading for a good child (`round.ts:92-104`). Weka and Quick looks are the best models of per-child fading.
3. **The 5 second rule can trap careful children** (every game using `shared/maths/leitner.ts` `afterAnswer`). A fact only moves up when it is right within `QUICK_MS`. In Hop the clock starts while the voice is still reading. `afterAnswer` already takes a `quickMs` argument, so each game can pass a junior value and start the clock after the voice, with no change to `shared/maths`.
4. **Teachers can't see who is stuck (all 7).** Per-fact data is saved (`gem_keep_facts` early-facts packs, `training_answers` for Quick looks) but there are no Today alerts and no class grid. Quick looks has no teacher view (`teacher_training_student` exists but isn't called). Beanstalk's Maths column says "Bonds to 10" even on the 100 and 1000 beanstalks.
5. **Short ladders with no "what's next" (Penguin Pairs, Tūī, Beanstalk).** Each game stops at its own top level, with no link to the next idea (for example Tūī to Weka, Pipi to Beanstalk).
6. **Strengths to keep.** Voice read-aloud, bright themes and calm pacing (clarity 3 everywhere); real representations (ten-frames, number lines, pebbles); server-checked answers; no timers, ranks or penalty.

**Shared components worth building once** (each is used by several fixes in section 8):
- **A misconception-aware wrong-answer maker.** Takes a fact and returns wrong choices each tagged with the mistake they stand for (one off, stopped at ten, reversed digits, n times n). It replaces the shared pack's "answer plus or minus 1" in all six games and gives R5 its reason lines.
- **A "show why" helper.** One component that re-draws the same picture (ten-frame, number line, pads) with the child's wrong choice marked, plus a voice line per mistake tag, and a cap on free taps followed by a guided redo.
- **A per-child help level.** Copy Weka's rule: help goes down after quick right answers and comes back after a wrong one, saved per fact, replacing the calendar bronze/silver/gold in four games (R6).
- **A junior timing rule.** One `quickMs` value for 5 to 8 year olds and a "clock starts after the voice" helper (R2).
- **A teacher Foundational view.** One class grid (stuck, secure, not played) and Today alerts reading the same `gem_keep_facts` rows, with Quick looks wired in (R7).
- **A "what's next" card.** A link at the top of each game's ladder to the next game (R8).

## 9. Limits of this review

- **What was played and what was read.** Weka, Tūī, Pipi, Penguin Pairs, Beanstalk and Hop were played in the browser preview, with deliberate wrong taps and jumps to later days. Weka's last two gold-day goes (hop-backs) were judged from the code. Weka's pace slowed in the scripted browser, so a full level-2 or gold round was not completed. Quick looks was played, but the flash is too fast for tools to catch, so pictures, buttons and feedback were checked by script and screenshot, and the stage and flash logic from code. The coverage matrix was built from code and shared files only; no game was played for it. Whether Bond Match's fraction cards show fraction pictures (which would make it C, not P, for halves) needs a play-through.
- **Previews are not real classrooms.** Taps were scripted, with no real 5 to 8 year olds watched. The preview Penguin Pairs ran on an in-browser trial backend with the same rules as the server. Timing effects (the 5 second rule, Penguin Pairs sharing animation, Tūī's 3.4 second hold) come partly from code, not stopwatch measurements.
- **Scores are judgement.** They were calibrated across games against the rubric and finished by the main session, and the helpers' first drafts differed in four places (section 2). A score of 4 on every line is not expected.
- **Research caveats.** Most learning-science studies used older children or university students, so for ages 5 to 8 the direction is clear but numbers (85 percent success, 5 to 10 minute sessions, 64 pixel targets) are guides, not rules. The 85 percent rule comes from computer models and simple tasks, not classroom children. I could not confirm some papers in full text (Sweller 1988, Shute 2008 guidelines, Rohrer and Taylor 2007), and [S21] (Fyfe and colleagues) is unverified. The 2025 curriculum pages on Tāhūrangi returned "403 Forbidden", so Phase 1 and Phase 2 year placements are "typical" ones, not quoted from the official page; please check against the official site. Askew and Van de Walle's textbooks could not be found online, so claims from them rest on secondary summaries and professional judgement. Ratings in the research map of skills are professional judgement.
- **Small, young live data.** The games are 1 to 6 days old, with 10 to 48 pupils per figure. The live numbers show accuracy is healthy, not whether children are being stretched. Re-run in 3 to 4 weeks. The exact live-query SQL was not saved, so the appendix queries may not reproduce the snapshot exactly.
- **Gap ranking.** The helper ranks were mostly tied at 9. The final ranking is a main-session judgement using the three tie-breaks in section 7.2. If equal groups and arrays are read as lower game-fit, ranks 4 to 6 could move.

## Appendix. SQL to re-run (from the game evaluations)

All queries are read-only selects. Run them through the Supabase Management API with the keychain token, not the CLI. Check column names on `profiles` (`display_name` or `full_name`) before running.

### Beanstalk

Source: `docs/reviews/maths-review/games/beanstalk.md`. Not run when written.

```sql
-- 1. Where is everyone? Days done, beanstalk number, and when they last played.
select p.display_name, b.child, b.days_done,
       b.days_done / 30 as eggs, (b.days_done % 30) as day_on_this_beanstalk,
       (array[10,100,1000])[1 + (b.days_done / 30) % 3] as this_beanstalks_sum,
       b.last_leaf_day
from public.beanstalk_progress b
join public.profiles p on p.id = b.player
order by b.last_leaf_day nulls first, b.days_done;

-- 2. Mistakes per finished challenge, per pupil (are they guessing?). A pupil who needs 10 right pairs has
--    taps = 10 + mistakes; high mistakes per round, or lots of rounds under 20 seconds, means tapping.
select p.display_name,
       count(*) as rounds,
       round(avg(r.mistakes), 1) as avg_mistakes,
       max(r.mistakes) as worst_round,
       round(avg(extract(epoch from (r.finished_at - r.started_at))), 0) as avg_seconds
from public.beanstalk_rounds r
join public.profiles p on p.id = r.player
where r.finished_at is not null
group by p.display_name
order by avg_mistakes desc;

-- 3. Which bonds are weakest across the school (hub fact records for bond10).
select f.fact_key, count(*) as pupils, sum(f.attempts) as attempts,
       round(100.0 * sum(f.correct) / nullif(sum(f.attempts), 0), 0) as pct_right,
       round(avg(f.avg_ms)) as avg_ms
from public.gem_keep_facts f
where f.fact_key like 'bond10:%' or f.fact_key like 'bond100:%' or f.fact_key like 'bond1000:%'
group by f.fact_key
order by pct_right;
```

### Weka

Source: `docs/reviews/maths-review/games/weka.md`. Not run when written.

```sql
-- 1. Where is each pupil? Level reached, days played, last played.
select p.display_name, w.reached as level, w.days_done, w.last_stone_day, w.updated_at
from public.weka_progress w join public.profiles p on p.id = w.player
order by w.reached, w.days_done;

-- 2. Error rate by fact, across all finished rounds (first-pick wrong rate; goes
--    stored per round, taps in order, so this uses mistakes per round as a rough guide).
select r.level, count(*) as rounds, avg(r.mistakes)::numeric(4,1) as avg_mistakes,
       count(*) filter (where r.mistakes >= 6) as rounds_6plus_mistakes
from public.weka_rounds r where not r.open
group by r.level order by r.level;

-- 3. Stuck pupils: on the same level for 5+ rounds, or facts still in box 0-1 after 6+ tries.
select p.display_name, w.reached, w.days_done, g.fact_key, g.attempts, g.correct, g.box
from public.gem_keep_facts g
join public.profiles p on p.id = g.player
join public.weka_progress w on w.player = g.player
where g.pack = 'add-sub' and g.attempts >= 6 and g.box < 2
  and g.fact_key ~ '^add:[1-9]\+[1-9]$'
  and ((substring(g.fact_key from 5 for 1))::int + (substring(g.fact_key from 7 for 1))::int) between 11 and 18
order by g.correct::numeric / g.attempts, g.attempts desc;
```

### Quick looks

Source: `docs/reviews/maths-review/games/quick-looks.md`. Not run when written.

```sql
-- 1. Where each pupil is: stages finished, start stage, and guided mode (Quick looks only)
select p.display_name, (s.typing->'q'->>'s')::int as start_stage, (s.typing->'q'->>'f')::int as stages_finished,
       (s.typing->'q'->>'g')::int as guided, s.updated_at
from training_students s join profiles p on p.id = s.player
where s.typing ? 'q'
order by stages_finished desc, p.display_name;

-- 2. Accuracy by stage-type, last 30 days, counted (not helped) answers only
select split_part(fact_key, '-', 1) as picture, count(*) as answers,
       round(100.0 * avg(correct::int)) as pct_right
from training_answers
where fact_key ~ '^(sub|gap):' and answered_at > now() - interval '30 days'
group by 1 order by pct_right;

-- 3. Stuck pupils: lots of helped answers and low accuracy recently
select p.display_name,
       count(*) filter (where a.fact_key ~ '^(sub|gap)h:') as helped,
       count(*) filter (where a.fact_key ~ '^(sub|gap):') as counted,
       round(100.0 * avg(a.correct::int) filter (where a.fact_key ~ '^(sub|gap):')) as pct_right
from training_answers a join profiles p on p.id = a.player
where a.fact_key ~ '^(sub|gap)h?:' and a.answered_at > now() - interval '14 days'
group by p.display_name
having count(*) filter (where a.fact_key ~ '^(sub|gap):') >= 20
order by pct_right nulls first;
```
